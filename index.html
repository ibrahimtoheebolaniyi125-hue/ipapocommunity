LIVING HERITAGE

Traditions that keep Ipapo connected

Explore the festivals, symbols and community values that carry Ipapo's identity from one generation to the next.

01
🎭

Egungun Festival

Ancestral remembrance expressed through masquerade, music, dance, colour and family gathering. It honours those who came before and brings families together in shared memory.

02
🌙

Oro Festival

A solemn traditional observance connected with communal order, respect, protection and quiet reflection. Its customs encourage discipline, responsibility and respect for the wider community.

03
⚡

Sango Festival

A celebration of courage, justice, thunder, drumming and the enduring strength of Yoruba heritage. Songs, movement and storytelling keep the values of bravery and fairness alive.

04
🌿

Sanda Festival

A place to document local stories, family traditions and the community meaning of Omo Sanda. It connects family memory, identity and cultural continuity across generations.

COMMUNITY ARCHIVE

Where the story of Ipapo begins

Ipapo's history should be preserved through the voices of elders, families, palace records and community archives.

A HISTORY TO VERIFY TOGETHER

From first settlement to community identity

Ipapo's founding account belongs to its oral traditions and family histories. This archive will bring together the earliest settlement stories, the names of founding families, migration routes, the development of quarters, and the institution of traditional leadership.

We will publish the final timeline after it has been reviewed with elders, the palace, historians and community associations. That keeps the story accurate, respectful and owned by the people of Ipapo.

Origins: founding families and first settlement accounts.
Growth: quarters, markets, farms, schools and community institutions.
Legacy: the Eleyinpo stool, festivals, crafts and the people shaping Ipapo today.
